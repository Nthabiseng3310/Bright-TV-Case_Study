## 📺 BrightTV Viewership Analytics Case Study

## 📌 Project Overview

BrightTV is a viewership analytics case study focused on understanding subscriber behaviour and television consumption patterns. This project analyzes user profile and viewing session where each row represents one viewing session and translate those findings into actionable recommendations, delivered as a 20-minute stakeholder presentation.

Dataset snapshots:

10,000 viewing session records across 4,386 unique subscribers

Period covered: January – March 2016

1,523 total hours of viewing 

Fields included subscriber demographics (sex, ethnicity, region, age group)

session-level detail (watch date/time, duration, TV channel, day/time classification)

The case study also specified that dates and times supplied in UTC should be converted to **South African Standard Time (SAST)**

# 🎯 Project Objectives

The main objectives of this project were to:

* Analyse BrightTV's subscriber and viewing behaviour.
* Understand the demographic profile of BrightTV users.
* Identify viewing and consumption trends.
* Analyse viewing behaviour by day, time and month.
* Identify peak and low-consumption periods.
* Analyse TV channel performance.
* Investigate the relationship between demographics and consumption.
* Identify highly engaged and low-engagement audience segments.
* Identify opportunities to increase viewing frequency and watch time.
* Recommend content strategies for low-consumption periods.
* Recommend initiatives that could support subscriber growth.
* Build an interactive Power BI dashboard.
* Present the findings in a clear, business-focused 20-minute presentation.

# 💡 Key Insights

The major findings from the analysis were:
1. BrightTV has a substantial young audience
The **Young Youth** age group was the largest audience segment and generated the highest total watch time.

2. Gauteng is a key market
Gauteng generated the highest number of unique viewers and total watch hours.
BrightTV could consider Gauteng an important market for targeted engagement and acquisition initiatives.

3. Live and event-based content performs strongly

**ICC Cricket World Cup 2011** and **Live_Events** generated the highest total watch hour

4. Afternoon and evening are important viewing periods
The **17:00 hour** was particularly strong

5. Monday represents a potential engagement opportunity
Monday had the lowest total watch hours among the full days represented.

6. Weekend sessions tend to be longer
Although weekdays produced more total sessions in the dataset, weekend sessions had a higher average duration.

7. March had the highest recorded consumption
 March generated approximately **839 watch hours**, significantly more than January and February.

 8. Low-usage viewers represent an engagement opportunity

The screen-time analysis identifies a large number of low/no-usage records.
BrightTV can use these segments to develop targeted engagement and reactivation strategies.

---

# 🚀 Recommendations

Based on the analysis, the following recommendations were developed.

1. Strengthen Live & Event-Based Programming
The strong performance of **Live_Events** and **ICC Cricket World Cup 2011** suggests that live and event-based programming can be an important engagement driver.

BrightTV should consider:

* Increasing promotion of major live events.
* Building campaigns around sports and entertainment events.
* Using high-performing event content to attract new viewers.
* Creating viewing reminders before major events.

---
 2. Develop a Monday Engagement Strategy

Monday had the lowest full-day consumption.

BrightTV could introduce:

* Monday content premieres.
* Monday-only promotions.
* Personalised recommendations.
* Popular weekend content catch-ups.
* Short-form promotional campaigns.

The objective would be to move Monday consumption closer to stronger-performing days.
